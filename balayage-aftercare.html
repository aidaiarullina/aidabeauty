How to Make Balayage Last Longer

By Aida • Hairstylist & Makeup Artist in Dublin, CA

Balayage is popular because it grows out softly and usually needs less frequent full-color appointments than traditional highlights. But “low maintenance” does not mean “no maintenance.” Lightened hair can become dry, warm, or brassy over time, especially when it is exposed to hot tools, frequent washing, sun, and pool water.

The good news is that a simple routine can help your balayage stay glossy, blended, and healthy between appointments.

Wait before washing

After your balayage appointment, wait at least 48 hours before your first shampoo, unless your stylist gives you different instructions. This gives the fresh color and toner time to settle.

When you do wash your hair, use lukewarm rather than very hot water. Hot water can leave hair feeling drier and may make the tone fade faster.

Use gentle, color-safe products

Choose a shampoo and conditioner made for color-treated hair. If your hair does not become oily quickly, washing two or three times per week may be enough. Focus shampoo mainly on the scalp and let the foam rinse through the lengths instead of scrubbing the lightened ends aggressively.

Many colorists recommend shampoos made for color-treated hair, such as Wella Professionals ColorMotion+ Shampoo, which is specifically designed to help lock in color while protecting smoothness and shine.

For clients who want extra smoothness and shine, I often suggest Wella Professionals ULTIMATE SMOOTH Shampoo, which is formulated to be color-safe and nourishing with Squalane and Omega‑9.

For dry or lightened ends, use a weekly conditioning mask. Balayage often involves lightening, so keeping the hair moisturized helps it look smoother and more reflective.

Protect your hair from heat

Blow-drying, straightening, and curling can make lightened hair look dry and dull. Apply a heat protectant before using hot tools, choose a lower heat setting when possible, and avoid holding a tool on one section for too long. Never use a curling iron or straightener on wet hair.

A simple habit makes a difference: air-dry when you can, then use heat only where you need extra polish.

Control unwanted brassiness

Blonde or lighter balayage can become warm between appointments. Purple shampoo can help neutralize yellow tones because purple sits opposite yellow on the color wheel. Use it only as needed—often once or twice a month is enough for many blondes—and follow with conditioner or a mask if your hair feels dry.

Purple shampoo is not the same as a salon toner. If your color looks orange, uneven, or much warmer than you want, ask your stylist for advice instead of using purple shampoo more and more often.

Protect it from sun and water

Sun exposure and chlorinated water can affect how colored hair feels and looks. Wear a hat during long periods in direct sun, or use a leave-in product with UV protection. Before swimming, wet your hair with clean water first; this can reduce how much pool or hot-tub water the hair absorbs.

Book a gloss or toner refresh

You may not need a full balayage appointment every month. A toner or gloss appointment can refresh shine and correct unwanted warmth without re-lightening the hair. Many stylists suggest a refresh around 6–8 weeks, while full balayage maintenance may often be needed closer to 12–16 weeks—but the best timing depends on your original color, your desired brightness, and your hair condition.

Final thought

The best balayage aftercare is not about buying every product. It is about using a few suitable products consistently, protecting the lightened areas, and asking your stylist for a routine based on your own hair.

Quick checklist

  • Wait 48 hours before your first wash.
  • Use gentle color-safe shampoo and conditioner.
  • Add a weekly mask.
  • Use heat protectant every time you style with heat.
  • Use purple shampoo only when needed.
  • Protect hair from sun and chlorine.
  • Book a toner or gloss when your color needs a refresh.
